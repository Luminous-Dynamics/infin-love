## What is a Gift Circle?

### The Basics

A **gift circle** is a community based on **gifting** rather than **exchanging**. Here's the difference:

**Traditional Exchange:**
- I give you X, you give me Y
- Transactional, balanced, quid pro quo
- Obligation-based
- Keeps score

**Gift Circle:**
- I give freely, without expectation
- You receive and pass forward when moved
- Trust-based, flow-oriented
- No scorekeeping

### Sacred Reciprocity

**The Principle:**
> "What we give to the world, the world gives back—not always through the same hands, but always through the great circle of life."

**In Practice:**
- Give your gifts freely
- Receive with gratitude
- Pass forward when able
- Trust the abundance of the circle
- No one owes anyone
- Everyone is enriched

### How It Works Here

**In the Infin.Love community:**

1. **Contributors give** their time, skills, ideas, support
2. **The community receives** and benefits from these gifts
3. **Members pass forward** by helping others, creating, sharing
4. **The circle grows** and everyone is nourished

**Examples:**
- Someone fixes a bug (gives) → Site improves (receive) → User shares with friend (pass forward)
- Someone asks question (gives vulnerability) → Others answer (give knowledge) → Asker helps next person (pass forward)
- Someone donates via Ko-fi (gives money) → Project sustained (receive) → Free for all (pass forward)

## Starting a Local Gift Circle

### What is a Local Circle?

A group of people in the same geographic area who:
- Meet regularly (weekly, monthly)
- Practice sacred reciprocity
- Share gifts, skills, resources
- Support each other
- Study and learn together

### How to Start One

#### Step 1: Gauge Interest

**Where to look:**
- Your existing community
- Friends and family
- Local organizations
- Social media groups
- Community boards
- Faith communities

**How to invite:**
> "I'm starting a gift circle to practice sacred reciprocity—giving freely, receiving gratefully, and supporting each other beyond money and transactions. Interested in joining?"

#### Step 2: Set Foundation

**First gathering:**
- Share why you're here
- Read about sacred reciprocity together
- Discuss hopes and intentions
- Agree on basic practices
- Decide on meeting rhythm

**Core agreements:**
- Confidentiality (what's shared stays)
- Respect and kindness
- No pressure to give
- Honor all gifts equally
- Show up authentically

#### Step 3: Create Structure

**Meeting format:**
- Check-in round (how is everyone?)
- Sharing circle (needs, offers, gratitude)
- Group activity (learning, planning, doing)
- Closing (appreciation, next steps)

**Frequency:**
- Weekly: Deep bonds, high commitment
- Bi-weekly: Sustainable, consistent
- Monthly: Lighter touch, easier scheduling

**Location:**
- Someone's home (rotate hosts)
- Community center
- Library meeting room
- Park (weather permitting)
- Online (for accessibility)

#### Step 4: Practice Together

**Gift sharing:**
- Share skills (teaching, repairing)
- Share resources (tools, books, food)
- Share time (helping with projects)
- Share wisdom (advice, experience)
- Share presence (listening, witnessing)

**No money needed:**
- Gifts are freely given
- Receiving is also a gift
- No tabs kept
- Trust the abundance

#### Step 5: Grow & Evolve

**Natural growth:**
- Members invite trusted friends
- Word spreads organically
- Circle may split when too large
- Cells multiply

**When challenges arise:**
- Talk openly and honestly
- Return to core principles
- Seek outside facilitation if needed
- Remember: conflict can deepen trust

## Communication Guidelines

### Healthy Communication

**Do:**
- Be kind and respectful
- Listen to understand
- Ask clarifying questions
- Acknowledge others' views
- Admit when you're wrong
- Apologize and repair
- Assume good intent
- Speak from "I" (not "you should")

**Don't:**
- Personal attacks
- Dismissive language
- Passive aggression
- Sarcasm at others' expense
- Talking over or silencing
- Demanding or entitled tone

### Conflict & Disagreement

**Conflict is normal:**
- Disagreement isn't failure
- Different perspectives enrich us
- Conflict can deepen connection
- Working through it builds trust

**How we handle conflict:**
1. **Address directly** (with kindness)
2. **Listen deeply** (seek to understand)
3. **Find common ground** (shared values)
4. **Explore solutions** (together)
5. **Seek help if needed** (mediator)

**Restorative approach:**
- Focus on repair, not punishment
- Understand impact and intent
- Make amends when harm done
- Restore relationships
- Learn and grow

**See:** [CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) for full process
